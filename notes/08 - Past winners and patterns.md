# Past global winners: what to learn

## How the global awards work
- The local event nominates *Global Nominees* → experts from NASA and partner agencies choose the *Global Finalists* → an executive committee picks **10 winners**.
- There are **10 awards**: Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, **Best Mission Concept** ("the most plausible concept and design"), Most Inspirational, Best Use of Storytelling, Global Connection, **Art & Technology** and Local Impact.
- In 2025 there were ~11,500 projects submitted by ~18,800 teams. Competition is tough: the project must be **clearly the best in its challenge** and fit an award.

## Winners relevant to us
| Year | Award | Project (country) | What it did | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | **Best Mission Concept** | **PureFlow** (Aracaju, Brazil) | Systems engineering platform: design and 3D-model a space habitat, then **test it against real space weather threats** (solar storms). Covers waste, power, life support and communications | **Design → test against real data → see if it survives.** That is our loop. And it is Brazilian |
| 2025 | Art & Technology | Zumorroda-X (Egypt) | Mini-games where the player is a farmer travelling the world and adapting to heat waves and floods | A game with a clear role and a journey; strong art |
| 2025 | Best Use of Science | SpaceGenes+ (Germany) | Interactive dashboard on radiation and microgravity | Visualization makes complex science accessible |
| 2024 | Most Inspirational | Eco-Metropolis (UAE) | City-building game with decisions based on **real environmental data**; **levels for different ages** | Real data inside the decision + difficulty levels |
| 2024 | Best Use of Science | SkyShield (Canada) | Web app of risk zones for satellites with real near-object data | Correct, visible science |
| 2022 | Best Mission Concept | Mars 3D Home | Mars habitat concept | Plausibility weighs heavily in Best Mission Concept |
| 2021 | Honorable Mention / finalist | SocialSpacing (Space Quest: The Game) | A 2021 challenge almost identical to ours (mission architecture game) | The theme has produced finalists before; we must stand out |

The 2021 challenge "Space Quest: The Game" suggested: define the objective → choose the destination → choose the architecture → explore, and give the player a fixed budget. Other teams that year used a planning phase + an execution phase (e.g. "Decks to Orbit", with mass and fuel as limits). **Plan-then-execute is the minimum expected; it is not enough to win.**

## Patterns (our inference; the sites don't publish the reason for each award)
1. **Real data or tools inside the decision**, not decorating the screen (PureFlow, Eco-Metropolis, SkyShield).
2. **Design → test → understand why it failed loop.** "Concept" winners validate the design against real threats.
3. **Clear audience and purpose**: students, levels by age, classroom.
4. **Working prototype with a public link.** A third-party guide that analyzed 10 years of winners recommends: a working prototype with a URL, **citing exact datasets** (name and version) instead of "we used NASA data", and polished documentation (README, long description). That guide is unofficial and has inconsistencies in the numbers, but the tips match the criteria.
5. **Art and narrative count** (there are awards just for that).
6. **Brazil wins**: PureFlow (2025) and three Brazilian winners in 2024 (Maceió, Campinas, Florianópolis).

## What we can aim for
- **Best Mission Concept** (main): plausible mission design, validated in simulation.
- **Art & Technology**: pixel art + the grid adjacency mechanic.
- **Most Inspirational / Storytelling**: classroom team mode + a report that explains failures.
- **Best Use of Science**: if the tables are well anchored in real missions and the post-mission analysis explains the physics.
